Seven questions to ask before you pay anyone

So here are the questions that tell them apart. Ask whoever you chose. If they answer these well, you picked a good one and I'll genuinely be happy for you.

1Who is actually training you — and what have they done?

Not who ran the ad. Who trains you.

  • How long have they personally sold in this industry — not taught it, done it?
  • How much revenue have they actually closed?
  • Do they train you directly, or does that cost more?
  • Are they an operator, or an influencer with a media budget?
Ours: Chad. Roughly $28 million in revenue closed, mentored by Tony Robbins, best-selling author on Amazon. He trains twice a week, personally, and he's the one who grades your final call and certifies you. Not a coach who's six months ahead of you.
2How do you actually get placed?

This is the question. Ask them to walk it step by step.

  • Is it one-on-one placement, or a job board?
  • If it's a board — how many of those companies could you find free on Indeed, LinkedIn, or a Facebook group?
  • Do you know which ones are actually hiring? Which are legitimate? Which want a closer versus a cold caller?
  • Do you pay a monthly fee to keep access to the board?
  • How long does it typically take — and do they have an average, or just a story?
Ours: we place you one-on-one with companies in our network of 300+, as many as you want. I don't know of another company in this space that does one-on-one placement — if you find one, that's genuinely worth knowing and I'd want to hear about it.

What that looks like in practice: one of my members did 17 interviews — not applications, interviews. Another had 12 offers of interviews inside 3 days and finished 6 first rounds, all in the exact field he asked for.

Average time to land: 38 days for closers. 23 days for setters.
3Is placement guaranteed — in writing?

Not "we'll work with you until you land it." That's a feeling. Ask for the actual written language.

  • What happens if you're not placed?
  • What if you get placed and it doesn't work out — do they replace you free, or do you pay again?
  • Is coaching and placement support lifetime, or is there a monthly charge to stay in?
Ours: If you complete your part and do not secure a qualifying role within 6 months of certification (or at least 50 interviews completed), you may qualify for a full refund (documentation required). Lifetime placement, no recurring fee. If a placement doesn't work out, we replace you at no cost.
4What does training actually look like?
  • Self-paced, or are you locked into 12 weeks?
  • Who do you practice with — other brand-new people, or something real?
  • How do you know when you're actually ready?
Ours: you practice on a real $12,500 offer, running full calls as if you were already their closer — and you're graded on every call, every category. You can build your own offer in it too. There's an AI roleplay system with five levels that get progressively harder, so you've already met the difficult buyers and the ugly objections before you meet a real one.

The point is that you produce in week one — instead of taking a month to find your feet and getting cut for not producing.
5Can you see real proof — and talk to real people?
  • Video testimonials, or just names on a page?
  • Are the companies verifiable and are they top-tier?
  • If you ask to speak to someone they placed, do they say yes — or do they get defensive and ask why you'd need that?
Ours: our people are at Tony Robbins, Alex Hormozi, Grant Cardone, John Maxwell, Taylor Conroy, Samantha Skelly. And if you want to talk to someone we placed, just say the word. I'll send you as many as you want. That question should never make anyone uncomfortable.
6What do the bad reviews say?

Not "are they good." What do the bad ones say.

Ours: Trustpilot, public and unedited — I can't remove one of them. Our lowest review is a 3-star, and what it says is that it took more effort than he expected. Which, honestly, it should — if the plan is six figures.
