03

Domains and hosting—in plain English

Domain = the address, such as acmeroofing.com. Hosting = the computer/platform serving the site. Website = the pages and files. They are three separate things.

A. Cheap preview you control

Use an agency-owned demo domain or preview address, such as acme.youragencydemo.com.

  • Purpose: show the prospect what you can build.
  • You own the generic demo domain.
  • Do not present it as their permanent official site.
  • Do not buy a domain containing their trademark without written permission.
→

B. Paid client site they own

The client buys and owns their branded domain. They invite you to manage DNS, and you point it to the website you host.

  • Client remains domain registrant and primary owner.
  • You own reusable templates; client owns approved business content.
  • Your monthly plan pays for hosting and services.
  • If they leave, the contract states what is transferable.
The safest two-product setup: Product 1 is a temporary preview on your generic domain. Product 2 is a managed website on the client’s domain. You do not need to own a roofer’s branded domain to host and manage their site.
DNS

What “connecting the domain” means

  1. Client purchases the domain in their own registrar account.
  2. Client gives CJ delegated access or joins a screen-share—never collect a shared master password.
  3. CJ copies the A/CNAME/TXT records shown by the hosting platform into the domain’s DNS settings.
  4. The domain stays owned by the client while pointing to the agency-hosted site. SSL/HTTPS is then enabled.

01

How to actually build the sites

The "AI reads their LinkedIn/social and builds it" tool you heard about isn't really a single mainstream product yet — what exists is AI builders that generate from a text description or business category, then you drop in their real name, photos, and reviews. For an agency doing this repeatedly, the faster move is a pre-built niche template you customize, not a from-scratch AI generation each time.

Build inside GoHighLevel Recommended for the pilot

  • Use a pre-built niche "snapshot" (roofing, med spa/wellness, etc.) — funnel-style site + pipeline + automations import together
  • Hosting is included in your GHL plan — $0 marginal cost per site
  • Site, CRM, missed-call text-back, and AI receptionist live in one login — nothing to reconcile later
  • Weaker for long-form content/blog SEO than real WordPress

Real WordPress site (10Web / ZipWP)

  • AI-generates a full WordPress site from a prompt; you own the site outright, better for content-driven SEO
  • Managed hosting bundled in, roughly $10–23/mo per tier estimate — confirm exact tier-by-site-count on their pricing page before quoting a client
  • Position as your "Pro / SEO" tier for clients who want to own the asset and blog long-term
  • Lead capture still needs to connect back into GHL for the receptionist/speed-to-lead workflow

Practical build flow either way: pull the business's real name, logo, photos, service list, and reviews from their Google Business Profile and Facebook/Instagram page yourself — that's 20 minutes of copy work, not a tool problem. Save every finished site as a duplicate-able snapshot/template per niche so site #6 takes an hour, not a day.

02

Finding businesses without a working web presence

Yes, this exists — it's a Google Maps scrape with a filter, not a mystery app.

Outscraper (or Apify's equivalent) pulls a Google Maps search — e.g. "roofers, Tampa FL" — with an "exclude businesses with a website" filter, returning name, phone, address, hours, and review count for every listing with no site or a dead one. Free tier to test; paid credits beyond that are pay-as-you-go estimate — check their calculator, exact per-record cost wasn't confirmed. That list is your first 100–300 cold-call targets before you touch dialing.
