7 · Honest summary

    Any question about any figure on this page is welcome. That is the reason it exists, so the decision gets made on arithmetic rather than a feeling in either direction.

    8 · The part the math cannot do

    Nothing changes until the pain of staying the same gets bigger than the pain of changing.

    Every number on this page came from answers they gave. The arithmetic can size the gap, price the delay, and count the weeks that are left. It cannot make the decision. That part has never been a math problem.

    Perception shapes decisions. Decisions shape actions. Actions shape results. If the way decisions have been getting made so far produced a job, an income, and a schedule that already got described as not what they want, then running the next decision through that same filter is very likely to produce more of the same. That is not a criticism. It is just how the loop works, and it is the one input on this page nobody else can change.

    What people who move quickly tend to have in common

    They decide fast once something makes logical sense, because they have learned that hesitation is itself a decision with a price. They spend their energy on how to make it work instead of only on what could go wrong. They are not more certain than anyone else. They have just stopped needing certainty before acting.

    The honest worst case

    The worst case is not zero, and it is worth saying plainly. Somebody in the slowest part of the pack takes most of a year to ramp and lands somewhere near $70,000 in a first year, while a fast starter can clear $150,000. Those describe placed members rather than a forecast for anyone specific, and the difference between them is almost never talent. It is consistency over months that are not exciting.

    Underneath even that: the ability to sell is not a job skill, it is a life skill. Getting hired, raising money, being taken seriously, defending an idea, all of it is selling. Learn it once and it does not expire, it does not get automated away, and it cannot be repossessed. Add lifetime access to the training and lifetime placement support, including help moving to a different company later, and the floor under this decision is considerably higher than it looks from the outside.

    The only question left

    Not whether the money makes sense. Section 5 already answered that. The real one is whether the next twelve months are going to look like the last twelve, and whether that is acceptable given how few weeks are actually left in Section 6.

    Let's finish this conversation properly

    Bring every objection, including the ones that feel rude. A decision made on real answers holds up. One made on enthusiasm does not.
