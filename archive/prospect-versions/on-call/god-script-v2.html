Section 2 · 5–7 min
Frame the call
Conversational, not corporate. Let them feel: we’re figuring out fit together — not pitching blind.
Why
Without this they think it’s an info call. You’re naming fit both ways — and that you’ll still point them right if it’s a no.
"So what these calls are all about — really — is to see if high-ticket sales makes sense for you, and if you'd be a good fit to get support from our team."

"To do that I want to dig a bit more into your background and your goals, and then we can see together if this is the right career path for you."

"And if I don't think it's the right fit — I'll shoot you straight and help point you in a better direction. But if it is a fit, I'll walk you through exactly how we help people get started in high-ticket sales and what the next steps would be to get you moving forward — at the end of this call."
"Sound fair?" Stop. Let them answer.
if"Just tell me the price" / "How does it work?" early▶
"I'll walk you through everything — the investment makes way more sense once I understand you first. Literally a few minutes. Can I ask you a couple quick things?"
Section 3 · 7–27 min
Discovery
Order: current reality → tenure + hours → what they still like → why they're looking → money + replacement target → pain → optional 3.6 reframes → heaven / gap. They talk ~80%. Do not open analogies before you earn them with basics first.
Rule
One question → silence. Start with facts before feelings: what they do, how long, how many hours, what they like, what they don't. A single day beats "tell me about your whole life." When you use an analogy from 3.6, tell it like a story, then stop.
3.1 Current reality — what they do, how long, what a day looks like
Application lead
"I see on your application you're listed as a [Realtor / college student / insurance agent / D2D rep / investment adviser / W2 / etc.] — but I'd rather hear it from you."
Setter lead
"I heard from your call with Nick that you're a [Realtor / college student / insurance agent / etc.] — but I want it in your words."
"What are you doing currently that has you looking in a different direction?" Silence.

"And how long have you been doing that?"

"Walk me through yesterday in that role — not your whole career story, just yesterday, start to finish, however you remember it." Mirror back the main beats in their words.
ifThey still drift into “my whole week” / life story▶
"I love the context — tighten it for me though. Pick one day last week, whatever's clearest — what did that day actually look like hour by hour or in main chunks?"
3.2 Baseline facts — hours, energy, what they still like
"About how many hours a day are you putting into that right now?"

"And if I asked what you still love about it — like if you could keep one part of your current job or situation — what would that be?" Silence.
ifThey seem to genuinely like the job / vibe is positive▶
"So you genuinely like [the piece they named]. Help me out then — if that's the part you want to keep, what's the part around it that's making you look elsewhere?"

"Why not just double down on [their current path] if the good part is still there?" (You're surfacing the gap, not attacking them.)
ifThey’re negative overall / already venting about the grind▶
"Oh, I got you — so if you could keep [what they still like], you would, but you're sick of [their words]. That makes total sense why you'd be looking for something different." → Then roll into 3.3 naturally.
3.3 Why they're looking — basic gap before the deeper pain
Gap
"So what is it about what you're doing currently that has you looking in a different direction in the first place?"
Then: "What started making this feel off?" → "How long have you known this probably isn't the long-term play?" → "Is it mostly money, freedom, fulfillment, burnout, or a mix?"
"And just so I understand the move — are you trying to get away from something specific, or are you mostly trying to get toward something bigger?"

"Which side is louder for you right now?"
3.4 Money reality — current income, replacement number, ceiling
"And right now, what are you actually bringing in from that — on average per month? Not your best month. The real average."

"If this transition worked the way you want it to, what number would you need to hit monthly for this to feel like a real win — or at least replace what you're making now?"

"And if nothing changed and you stayed on this track — where do you realistically see that income in the next twelve months? Same ballpark, a little more, or pretty capped?"
If the goal is high but their current model is capped
"Do the math with me for a second. If you want [their goal] and you're at [current] right now — how many more hours would you need at your current rate to even get close? Is that even possible without inventing new physics?" (Pause.)

"So if the math doesn't work there — the real question isn't whether you're willing to grind. It's whether the direction is broken. Fair?"
3.5 Pain dig — emotional reality after the basics are clear
Pain
"On the flip side — what do you not like about it that has you open to talking about something different?"
Then: "Give me a real example from the last thirty to sixty days where you felt that the most. What happened?" → "In that moment, what were you feeling?" → "What was the worst part for you?" → "How long has it felt like that — better, same, or worse?"
If they minimize pain — pick one (slow delivery)
"Let me ask it a different way — and I want you to actually think out loud with me. If nothing changes, what does this look like in two days… two weeks… two months… two years? Not for me — for you." (Say it slow. Let them answer each beat if you can.)

"Where does [their pain] go if we don't address it — does it shrink, or does it usually get heavier?"
"It's like brushing your teeth — skip one day, whatever. Skip a year, and the problem isn't 'I missed a day,' it's a whole different conversation. So I'm not being dramatic for fun — I want to know where this goes for you in six to twelve months if the pattern stays the same."
Tony line when it fits: "Tony Robbins says change happens when the pain of staying the same becomes greater than the pain of change — so what's the real cost if nothing changes in the next six months, for you personally, not just the money?" Then silence.

3.8 Cole-style frameworks · tailored to HTSA (not agency B2B)
Order of objections (mental model)
1 · Uncertainty — "Will this work for me?" / past bad course or job hunt. 2 · Logistics — spouse, money, timing. If you only handle logistics while they're still uncertain, you're often handling a smoke screen. Get real certainty on the path (train → certify → placement support) first.
Never say (weak)
"Why is that important to you now?" — sounds like a dart. Use permission → context → question below instead.
Use: when they're vague, "think about it," or you need to isolate certainty on the process before price/logistics.

"Hey — no problem, that's totally cool. We've covered a lot today — how the training works, certification, coaching with Chad, and Nate / placement on the backend. Outside of the money — do you feel like that path is what you actually need to get to [their goal: remote role / income / career change]?"

"Gotcha — so money aside, you're 100% in that this is the right move for you?"

If they say "I think so" — mirror it light: "'I think so' — what's really going on?" You want a real no or a real yes, not fog.

If the real block is past burn (another course, coach, job promise): isolate it — "So if we set that aside for a second, is that the only thing making you hesitate?" → then go to past-burn story + HTSA proof (Sec 4 / Sec 8 tabs).
Permission · context · question — better "why now" than "why is this important now."

"Can I ask you a personal question?" (Wait.)

Context (fill in their reality): "You've been [in corporate / in this industry / home with kids / in school] for [time]. By most people's standards that's [okay / respectable / a lot on your plate]."

"So after all that — why all of a sudden are you looking at high-ticket sales / a real career shift now? What shifted — what happened?" You're asking them to justify the change to themselves (consistency). Listen for the moment — that's urgency you can echo later.
Future-you — use in discovery when they can't name the pain cleanly yet, or before you reflect belief back.

"Can I ask you a weird personal question? I had this thing for a while — I felt like there's another version of me out there doing the life I actually want, but I couldn't figure out how to get there. I know that's weird — have you ever felt anything like that?"

"What has that been like for you?"

"What do you think it would feel like to actually get there?" → then bridge to: "Can I give you feedback on everything you've told me so far?" → your belief / fit monologue (you already have pieces in 3.6 + Discovery anchor).

HTSA belief line (B2B flavor adapted): "Getting the skill and the offer down — that's work, but it's not the mystery. What I'm actually excited about for you is [call out 2–3 traits they showed: coachable, hungry, already persuasive, etc.] — because that's what makes people win in remote high ticket with our placement support."
Supportive — overwhelmed parent, shame, fear: slow down. "This is a huge decision — I see that. I'm on your team. We're going to hold your hand through onboarding, coaching, placement — not throw you in alone." Ask: "Will you trust us enough to take the next step?"

Direct / push — prideful, "I already know sales," keeps deflecting: "Can I shoot straight for a second? Earlier you said you want [goal]. If nothing changes in your current path — is that goal actually happening, yes or no?" … "So is that goal real — or are we just talking about it?" … "You know what the right move is — are you going to do it, yes or no?" 50/50 they bristle or wake up — match volume to their personality.
When discovery is full → 4 · Story Bridge (one story, pen & paper, then Demo). Optional: blend a line from 3.6 into the handoff — “What you said about [pain] reminds me of…”

"So if I'm hearing you right — you want [their words]. What's between you and that is a decision and the right support. Fair?" → open Section 4 and match your story to their arc.
Section 4 · 27–30 min
Story Bridge
One story only. Match the closest arc, deliver it word for word, then tie it back to their pain and desired outcome before moving into the demo.
Why here
Emotion is highest after they painted pain + future. Tell one story like proof of concept, not like you are reading a testimonial page. The job is to make them feel: "someone like me did this, so maybe I can too."
Open: "Based on what you told me, you actually remind me a lot of [closest success story]."

Bridge line: "The reason I say that is because [their pain / situation / goal]."

Close the story with: "So hearing that, does that feel like the kind of shift you're trying to make too, or is your situation a little different?"
Pitch Mode
Formula: mirror their world → tell the story in plain English → land the takeaway line. Keep the energy conversational. You are not proving HTSA is amazing; you are proving their outcome is believable.

Section 5
Demo — Keep It Tight
~10 min. Tie-down after each block. Don’t lecture AI platform for 15 min.
Pitch stack (Cole-style) · HTSA version
When you explain the offer, run: mistake → consequence → what we do instead → benefit. Example: "One of the biggest mistakes people make in [their situation] is trying to job-board their way into high ticket without reps or positioning — the problem is they burn six months and lose confidence. What we do instead is train you, certify you, and put you in front of vetted opportunities with support. So the real benefit isn't a certificate — it's a skill + placement path that matches how remote closers actually get hired." Optional killer line: "When this is working, you're not trading hours for dollars — you're paid for a skill you can run from home." (Skill/career asset, not 'business runs without you' — that's coach-speak.)
Promise: Do the work + certified → we get you hired. 100% who finish + work with Nate land. Two objectives: (1) train you as a strong remote closer (2) get you in front of right companies. Make sense?
Cert + reps: ~50 lessons, 12–20 hrs, lifetime access. Real skill = reps. Messi — thousands of reps; AI roleplay, real scripts, graded feedback. How do you feel about reps vs only slides?
Live coaching + community: Chad on live calls, recordings, mastermind 24/7. Part-time $4–6k vs athletes at $30–40k+; avg ~$10–12k. Which do you see yourself if you took this seriously? Culture: help the person behind you. How important is high-performer support?
Mock + placement: Live mock, Chad signs off. Then Nate 1:1 — roadmap, resume, matched ops, apps pulled (not job-board roulette). Often <3 weeks. Lifetime access — course, coaching, community, placement help if you switch offers later. What stands out most — Chad, community, or Nate placement? → Sec 6–7.
