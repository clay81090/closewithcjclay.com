He has a Trameil Lee closing script he calls "not the best but ok." Beat it. That
document's structure is right:

   will break trust if raised. Be specific and blunt.
6. **THE TAILORED CLOSING SCRIPT** — phased, timed (`PHASE 1 — OPEN (0–2 min)`),

## 7. HARD RULES — every one of these came from a real mistake

1. **Never use a real person's name, phone, or email as test data.** This nearly
   sent a text to Amy. Use `Test Person` / `test@example.com` / `+15555550100`.
   If unsure whether a name is real, assume it is.
2. **Never put "reactivation" in a filename, URL, title, meta tag, or visible
   copy.** Internal word only. To them it's a special offer.
3. **No income guarantees. No "$10k/month."** A NotebookLM draft contained
   *"guarantee you hit a consistent $10,000 monthly income by Month 3."* That is an
   earnings claim. Removed. Say "a realistic target we set with you."
4. **Never make reviews a condition of a discount.** Trustpilot prohibits
   incentivized reviews and CJ's 4.9 rating is load-bearing across every page. A
   testimonial-for-discount trade is fine and disclosed. Third-party platform
   reviews are asked for separately, "only if you mean it."
5. **Never say "if it doesn't work out."** CJ's instruction. Frame as "once you've
   hit your milestones and you're ramped."
6. **No em dashes or en dashes in client texts and emails.** His own rule,
   `.cursor/rules/no-dashes-in-client-copy.mdc`. Commas and periods.
7. **Never write a negative descriptive norm about other prospects.** No "most
   people don't reply." It normalizes flaking and signals CJ gets ignored.
8. **Terms acceptance happens AFTER plan selection**, inside the confirm sheet.
   Never gate payment buttons behind a terms checkbox — that is what killed
   conversion on the old 10,351px enrollment page.
9. **Don't overload Chad.** Nate does the 90-day onramp. Call reviews go to "our
   coaching team." Chad's 1-on-1 is a **jumpstart session**, best used ~2 weeks
   into the offer.
10. **The lifespan chart stays.** CJ likes it. Don't propose cutting it again.

## 8. The live close process (CJ's actual workflow)

He does not want to take cards over the phone, and sending a link then hanging up
gives people time to talk themselves out of it. The process:

1. Get the verbal on **which plan** before sending anything.
2. "I'm texting you a page right now. Stay on with me, takes about 90 seconds."
3. Text it **while still talking**. Never send and hang up.
4. Narrate: *"Top green box says Paid in Full. Tap it."*
5. Terms sheet is one line: *"That's the terms and the guarantee, check the box,
   hit continue."*
6. Stay on the line through Whop. Talk about their start date while they type.
7. They text "payment confirmed," CJ turns on access while still on the phone.

The close page is built to be narrated — minimal content on purpose, so "tap the
green box" has one referent.

## 9. Design system (all pages share it)

```
navy #0a1628 · navy-soft #152a46 · ink #0f2237
green #61e38b · green-strong #00c97a · green-dark #087b50
Playfair Display (headings) + DM Sans (body)
```

**Nothing renders under 12px.** CJ shipped a page with 7–9px type and his audience
skews 40+. Body copy 14–15px. Check with a computed-style scan, not by eye.

**Watch for this CSS bug:** `display:flex` on an `<li>` turns every inline `<b>`
into its own flex column and shreds the layout. Use `position:relative` +
`padding-left` with an absolutely positioned `::before` marker instead. It bit
once already.
